For some people, a phone delivers over a hundred notifications a day. Almost none of them are urgent or important. Yet every buzz breaks your concentration and adds a small pulse of "I should deal with that" stress. That's notification overload.

The problem is that most apps **don't distinguish between "notifications you actually need" and "notifications they just want to send" — they send all of them.** So you have to draw the line yourself. It takes about ten minutes. Do it once and your phone gets dramatically quieter.

## Step 1: Sort notifications into three kinds

First, mentally split incoming notifications into three groups.

1. **Need to know now:** messages from people (calls, texts), payments, deliveries, reminders. → Keep.
2. **Fine to see later:** social reactions, news, app updates. → **Turn off. Open it yourself when you want to.**
3. **Never needed:** a game's "come back" nudge, sale promotions, recommendations. → **Off completely.**

Most notifications are group 2 or 3. Just silencing those two slashes how often your phone buzzes.

## Step 2: Turn notifications off per app

On Android, go to Settings → Notifications → App notifications and switch notifications off app by app. Work through everything you put in groups 2 and 3 above.

The single most effective move is **turning off all notifications from social apps.** You don't need to know about a "like" or a new follower the instant it happens — it's all there when you open the app. Stop being paged, and put yourself back in charge of when you look.

## Step 3: Fine-tune with notification categories

Many apps split their notifications into "categories" (a messaging app might have "new messages," "group join alerts," and so on). When cutting an app off entirely feels like too much but part of it is noisy, you can switch off just that category.

That lets you keep "I want the important messages, not the pointless alerts" — without deleting the app.

## Step 4: Go silent when you need to focus

Once the settings are done, the rest is about timing. Use Android's **Do Not Disturb (Bedtime / Focus mode)** to stop notifications entirely during work or sleep.

You can add exceptions like "let calls from specific people through," so there's no worry about missing something important. Even setting it for nighttime alone reduces the pre-sleep phone drift.

## Step 5: Make dismissed notifications reviewable

Once you cut notifications down, you may find yourself wondering "wait, what was that one?" Swiped-away notifications, in particular, are annoyingly gone for good.

As a safeguard, you can use an app like [Notikeeper](/en/apps/notikeeper/) that saves and organizes dismissed notifications so you can check them later. It lets you have both: fewer interruptions, without losing the ones that mattered.

## Flip your default to "off"

The most important part is the mindset. Most people assume "notifications are on by default, and I turn off the noisy ones." Flip it: **"notifications are off by default, and I turn on only what I truly need."**

Review your apps through that lens and you'll be surprised how few deserve to be on — roughly just messages from people and time-based things (reminders, calendar). Everything else, you can go and check yourself.

If you're also curious where your time actually goes, pairing this with a usage tracker like [AppClock](/en/apps/appclock/) lets you rethink your relationship with the phone from both the notification and the time angle.

## Wrapping up

Notification overload is solved by settings, not willpower. **Keep only the notifications you need, turn off the rest** — and switch to an "off by default" mindset.

Start by turning off notifications for your single noisiest app, right now. That bit of quiet is what sparks the next step.
